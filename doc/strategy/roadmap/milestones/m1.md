# M1: Safe To Try

## Description

M1 proves the first consumer-money loop for investing:

```text
have an idea
  -> see what it would do
  -> understand why
  -> feel safe enough to try something else
```

A user can turn an investment thesis into a basket, trade ticket,
affordability result, and paper trade. They then see how those proposals
affect cash, buying power, portfolio exposure, pending obligations, approval
state, and drift.

M0 established the trust foundation that makes this replayable. Payment
proposals live in M6. M1 is the investing baseline everything else builds on.

## Included Epics

Product increments:

- `V1.1`: From Idea To Paper Trade
- `V1.11`: The Trust Proof (governed boundaries, full audit,
  replay, no-bypass enforcement)
- `V1.3`: See Everything Before You Act

## Why This Is A Milestone

People have money and ideas about investing, but most don't act because the
cost of being wrong feels worse than the benefit of being right. They don't
need more information — they need a space where they can test their instincts
and learn from the results without consequence.

M1 gives them that space. A user with a money idea can try it, see what
would happen, understand why it was allowed or restricted, and feel safe
enough to try another. The loop is paper-trading — the experience is
building confidence in their own judgment.

This establishes the core product promise for the investing domain and
earns the right to call this a consumer-money product. It closes the
investing loop end to end:

- investment intent and paper trade construction
- cash, portfolio, obligation, approval, and drift impact

When M1 is complete, Tickoni has moved beyond a verifiable runtime to a user
product. The user has their first real experience of making a money decision
and understanding it — the foundation for trust in everything that follows.

### The Core Promise

The value of M1 isn't that the system can process investment ideas — it's that it can let someone *try* without paying for a mistake. That changes everything. Most investing tools answer questions that were never asked. This one answers the real question: *"Am I right about this?"* By turning a plain-English thesis into a concrete basket with affordability checks, clear rejection reasons, and paper execution, M1 gives the user a private proving ground where their own instincts can be tested against reality instead of against the fear of losing money.

### The Journey

The journey itself is the insight. A user types a plain-English thesis — *"I want exposure to AI infrastructure but avoid single-name concentration"* — and watches it become a concrete basket with reasons for each inclusion and rejection. The affordability check tells them not just "yes" or "no," but exactly how much they *can* afford and why the rest is out of reach. Restricted instruments are called out by name with the exact scope dimension that disqualified them. This isn't filtering; it's education through constraint. The user learns what their idea is actually worth before committing a single dollar.

Then the system shows the full picture: if this trade executes, what happens to sector exposure? How does cash change? Are there pending obligations that shift the picture? Before/after portfolio views, thesis cards, and proposal cards link every position back to its rationale. Drift signals watch for when the thesis breaches its own limits — not as a penalty, but as a check-in. The user sees that their investment idea doesn't live in a vacuum; it has weight, it has neighbors, and it has consequences.

### The Trust Signal

And then comes the trust signal: the part that does the heavy lifting that marketing copy can only try to emulate. No part of this flow can be gamed. Every step runs through governed boundaries — each tile validates before forwarding, each model call is scoped and audited, each paper placement binds to a validated ticket with a deterministic identity. Bypass attempts are blocked with explicit reasons. Audit trails record everything from thesis input to replay result. The replay is source-driven and fail-closed — tamper with the captured inputs and the system doesn't silently guess; it reports exactly which field and which sequence diverged.

The restriction itself is the product. Users don't trust systems they think can be worked around. A system that says *"you cannot"* and means it — even to itself — communicates something that no amount of reassuring language could ever achieve.

### What This Means

When M1 is complete, Tickoni has moved beyond a verifiable runtime to a real product. The user has experienced making a financial decision, understanding its full impact, and trusting that the boundaries protect them — not the other way around. These are the properties that separate a tool from a product, and a product from something people feel safe building wealth with over time.
