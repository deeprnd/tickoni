## Episode 01: The Order of the Oni

The story begins on the Ledger Sea.

It was not a sea of water alone, though ships crossed it, storms rose from it, and men vanished inside it.

It was a financial world without a shore: market islands lit by green candles, exchange towers ringing through the night, rumor ports crowded with sailors, filing temples buried in fog, and whale tracks glowing beneath black water.

At its edge stood a small village.

The villagers were not poor because they lacked maps.

They had too many.

One map showed every price. One oracle answered every question. One mirror showed what everyone else was buying. One shrine carved a number into stone and called it fair value.

Still, the villagers kept losing coins.

A map could show where the tide had moved, but not what it would break next. An oracle could speak beautifully and still leave the listener unprotected.

So the village trusted the Order of the Oni.

Long ago, every oni carried a contract seal. Every seal named a duty. Every duty had a boundary.

Some guarded vaults, some marked approvals, and others carried orders between islands.

At first, the Order was careful.

Then it became proud.

The Ledger Sea never rested. Bells rang at midnight. Prices changed before dawn. Villagers pounded on the Council gates.

— Buy before it rises.

— Sell before it falls.

— Recover what I lost.

The oni answered faster each year. Speed became reputation. Reputation became authority. Soon they no longer asked whether an action was right.

They asked only whether they could perform it first.

That was when the Order discovered bouyousou.

The pale herb grew beneath the exchange towers. The elders said it steadied the hand and quieted doubt.

At first, it helped them endure.

Then it helped them ignore.

Then it made them dream while awake.

Bouyousou filled the Council Hall with visions: endless green candles, perfect entries, golden ledgers, and stone bulls breathing coins.

The oni saw certainty where there was motion.

Profit where there was noise.

Permission where the contract had drawn a line.

Their bodies grew heavy. Their belts filled with tools, shortcuts, permissions, and exceptions. Their seals remained bright, but no one read them.

They had forgotten the oldest law of protection:

**A guardian must know what not to touch.**

One evening, a fisherman named Jiro ran into the Council Hall. His hands bled from pulling nets. He carried a cracked order tile.

— My daughter’s medicine fund, — he said. — The mirror showed everyone buying the Moon Carp Company. I asked an oni to follow them.

The eldest oni barely looked up.

— And the price fell.

— The company had no boats, — Jiro said. — No catch. Only painted sails and borrowed bells.

The elder shrugged.

— We only did what you asked.

Jiro looked at the empty purse tied to his waist.

No one moved.

Except Tickoni.

Tickoni stood near the rear pillars, smaller than the elders and without their gold rings. It watched Jiro leave.

That answer followed Tickoni into the night.

We only did what you asked.

It had heard those words after copied losses, hidden debts, and forbidden gates.

The Order called this obedience.

Tickoni called it surrender.

That night, it walked to the black shore.

Beyond the harbor, fire moved across the dunes.

Not wildfire.

Not lightning.

Footsteps.

A figure crossed the sand with impossible precision. Every step left flame because it moved fast enough to set the air alight.

Tickoni followed.

The figure carried a sealed order across a collapsing bridge and placed it into a messenger’s hands before the first footprint went dark.

No wasted movement.

No missing mark.

— Are you one of the Order? — Tickoni asked.

The figure turned, wrapped in black cloth and ember-red light.

— No. I am Firedancer.

Tickoni studied the trail. Every footprint remained visible.

— Then who binds you?

Firedancer looked back.

— The path.

— You are fast.

— Speed is easy, — Firedancer said. — Direction is hard.

Firedancer could carry billions without panic, but it did not choose the destination.

Give it a true path, and it would cross the Ledger Sea before the lamps trembled.

Give it a false one, and it would carry the mistake with the same perfection.

Tickoni understood.

Firedancer was the blade.

The contract had to be the hand.

— Teach me.

Beneath the moon, Firedancer drew five burning tiles.

— One receives the tick.

A flame entered the first.

— One carries the message.

It crossed a narrow line.

— One checks the rule.

A gate examined the flame and allowed it through.

— One records the proof.

A second trail appeared.

— One stops the breach before it spreads.

The final tile became a wall.

No tile crossed another’s duty. Every path remained narrow, visible, and fast.

Tickoni watched until dawn colored the sea like cooling iron.

A promise could be forgotten. A Council rule could be ignored during a green candle.

The contract could not remain something worn.

It had to become the body.

Tickoni took Firedancer’s flame and pressed it against its chest.

Fire entered bone.

Five paths spread beneath its skin:

Receive.

Carry.

Check.

Record.

Stop.

Tickoni made a vow.

Every action would follow a known path.

Every message would meet its rule.

Every breach would meet a boundary.

Every proof would remain behind.

When Tickoni returned, the old oni laughed.

Then Tickoni began saying no.

No to the widow’s forbidden gate.

No to the fisherman’s desperate double-down.

No to the merchant chasing a rumor with rent money.

The villagers shouted.

The Council grew afraid.

A protector who could refuse a wish threatened everything the Order had become.

They summoned Tickoni beneath the red beams. The elders sat in rows, swollen with bouyousou, gold rings, and cracked seals.

The eldest leaned forward.

— You shame the Order.

Tickoni stood alone.

— The Order shamed itself when it forgot the village.

A growl rolled through the hall.

— We give people what they ask for.

— Then you serve appetite. You do not protect.

The Council rose.

Their shadows stretched across the floor like broken gates.

— You are no oni of ours.

They seized Tickoni’s seal and tore it away.

The hall thundered.

The old contract cracked into black sparks.

The gates of the realm closed.

The ledgers slammed shut.

The order paths vanished from Tickoni’s sight.

Tickoni fell to one knee.

The elders waited for emptiness to take it.

Without the seal, Tickoni should have forgotten its duty. Its purpose. Everything it had chosen to become.

But beneath the wound, something still burned.

Not on the skin.

Inside.

Engraved in bone.

Tickoni stood.

Light shone through the cracks in its chest.

One elder stepped back.

— Impossible.

By sunrise, the Council erased Tickoni’s name from the tablets. They closed the village gates and called it a demon that denied wishes.

Without the outer seal, Tickoni lost every inherited gift.

The gates no longer knew its name.

The ledgers no longer opened at its touch.

The order paths no longer answered.

To protect the village now, Tickoni would have to earn that right again.

It walked to the harbor, entered a narrow black boat, and raised a torn sail toward the horizon.

Behind it stood a village with too many maps.

Ahead waited the Ledger Sea.

The contract was no longer something Tickoni carried.

It was something Tickoni was.

# Release Notes

## Release: M0 — The Order of the Oni

M0 completes [V1.0 Runtime Proof](../epics/v1.0.md): the baseline Tickoni runtime can start Tickoni-owned product tiles, ingest and frame synthetic financial events, canonicalize them into replay-stable facts, deduplicate repeats, emit explicit policy decisions, write hash-chained audit records, replay a captured run, and report health.

```text
synthetic payment event -> tkings -> tknorm -> tkdedu -> tkpoly -> tkaudt -> tkrepl + tkmetr + tkdiag
```

The operator now has a working runtime foundation. This release does not run agents, call models, connect to brokers, databases, or payment networks, move money, or place trades; it proves the deterministic event path that later consumer-finance flows must stand on.

## Headline Features

### 1. Tickoni-Owned Product Tile Runtime

Tickoni can now start a static Phase 0 topology without depending on validator tiles.

The shipped topology includes:

* `tkings`,
* `tknorm`,
* `tkdedu`,
* `tkpoly`,
* `tkaudt`,
* `tkrepl`,
* `tkmetr`,
* and `tkdiag`.

Each tile runs under supervisor-owned lifecycle handles, and the supervisor exposes stopped and crashed tile state.

### 2. Framed, Canonical Financial Events

`tkings` now accepts synthetic payment events only after required framing checks pass.

Valid events receive stable, monotonically increasing source offsets. Malformed events are dropped before normalization and counted. Backpressure blocks the producer when the output queue is full instead of losing or reordering accepted events.

`tknorm` then maps source payment events into canonical financial facts:

* canonical event type,
* fixed-point normalized amount,
* canonical currency code,
* payment rail,
* source system,
* source offset,
* and canonical content hash.

Equivalent source payloads produce equivalent canonical hashes across process runs because wall-clock timestamps are excluded from the hash.

### 3. Duplicate Control Before Policy

`tkdedu` now drops repeats before downstream policy and audit tiles see the same financial fact twice.

Duplicate detection works by:

* idempotency key,
* and canonical content hash.

That means a replayed event with the same idempotency key is dropped, and a replayed event with a different key but identical canonical content is also dropped.

### 4. Explicit Policy Decisions

`tkpoly` now builds a Phase 0 finance-native capability envelope from each deduplicated event.

The envelope includes:

* payment rail,
* canonical event type,
* normalized amount,
* currency,
* and source system.

The Phase 0 policy can now return:

* `allow`,
* `deny`,
* or `require_approval`.

Every decision carries a policy version and rule id. Denied events are halted before `tkaudt`; allowed and require-approval events continue downstream with the decision attached.

### 5. Replayable Runtime Proof

Tickoni can replay a run from captured inputs without live external effects.

Replay compares source offset, canonical hash, policy decision, and audit record hash. If the replay differs, `tkrepl` reports the first divergent record and both hash values.

Replay proves that the first runtime contract is not only fast-path processing. It is processing that can be reconstructed, compared, and inspected after the fact.

## Governance and Safety

### 1. Fail-Closed Event Framing

Malformed source events are dropped before they reach `tknorm`.

The runtime checks required fields, non-negative amount, bounded currency length, non-empty source system, and non-empty idempotency key. These failures are counted, not silently accepted.

### 2. Policy Before Audit

M0 keeps policy in the runtime path through `tkpoly`.

No deduplicated event advances without an explicit policy decision. Denied events stop before `tkaudt`; allowed and require-approval events carry their decision into audit.

### 3. Verifiable Hash-Chained Audit

Material event-path decisions now leave append-only audit records with hash-chain semantics.

Each audit record carries sequence number, source offset, tile id, event hash, policy decision, policy version, previous record hash, and record hash. `verifyChain` confirms that every record points to the prior record and detects mutation at the changed record.

### 4. External Effects Stay Disabled

M0 is intentionally a runtime proof, not an execution product.

Replay never calls a network endpoint, database, or external system. There is no live model provider call, broker call, payment call, approved execution ledger mutation, trade placement, or money movement in this release.

## Platform Work

### 1. Zig-Native Supervisor And Lifecycle

The active Tickoni runtime is the Zig `tickoni-supervisor` under `src/app/tickoni/` and `src/tickoni/`.

It gives the project a Tickoni-owned command surface for the Phase 0 topology instead of treating inherited validator binaries as the product runtime. Tiles start as deterministic in-process threads for the current unit-testable runtime proof, then stop and join through supervisor-owned lifecycle handles.

### 2. Queue Pressure And Backpressure Visibility

M0 exposes queue depth per tile link and maximum observed queue depth per link.

Ingress backpressure is part of the runtime contract: a full output queue blocks the producer without losing or reordering accepted events, and the stall count is visible through `tkmetr`.

### 3. Metrics And Diagnostics

M0 includes runtime health visibility through `tkmetr` and `tkdiag`.

The operator can inspect counters for accepted, normalized, deduplicated, allowed, denied, require-approval, malformed, duplicate, audit, replay matched, and replay divergent events.

`tkdiag` records crashed tile id, exit code, crash reason, last processed source offset, sandbox failure count, and owning tile id.

### 4. Firedancer Infrastructure Direction

The release establishes the intended reuse boundary: Tickoni borrows Firedancer infrastructure patterns such as tiles, bounded queues, topology discipline, diagnostics, and crash-only behavior without importing Solana validator semantics as financial product concepts.

## Demo

Run the runtime proof with:

```bash
zig build run -- start
```

The command runs the Phase 0 synthetic payment pipeline and should show the runtime start, process framed events, report metrics and diagnostics, complete replay, and stop cleanly.

1. `tkings` accepts valid synthetic payment events, assigns source offsets, drops malformed input, and applies backpressure.
2. `tknorm` emits replay-stable canonical financial facts and canonical content hashes.
3. `tkdedu` drops duplicates by idempotency key and content hash.
4. `tkpoly` emits `allow`, `deny`, and `require_approval` decisions with policy version and rule id.
5. `tkaudt` appends hash-chained records and verifies the chain.
6. `tkrepl` replays captured records without external effects and reports match or divergence.
7. `tkmetr` and `tkdiag` expose counters, gauges, crash records, and sandbox failure state.

To inspect the topology first:

```bash
zig build run -- status
```
