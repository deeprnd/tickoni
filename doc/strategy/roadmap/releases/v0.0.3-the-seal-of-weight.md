## Episode 03: The Seal of Weight

By sunset, the Ledger Sea had forgotten the storm.

The green lightning was gone. The oil ships had passed. Only their distant bells remained, fading beyond the horizon.

Maro sat beneath the mast of Tickoni’s narrow black boat, counting the coins in his purse.

Two thousand yen.

He closed his fingers around them.

For the first time, he was grateful the oil ticket had never become an order.

Yet he counted more slowly now.

Beside him lay another possibility.

During the afternoon, they had sailed past four islands linked by silver bridges: an ironworks whose furnaces burned without smoke, a cloud tower crowded with messengers, a cable maker threading light beneath the sea, and a broad technology fund that owned pieces of all three.

Maro had watched them until they disappeared behind the mist.

Now their names rested inside a fresh ticket.

Four positions.

No leverage.

No forbidden gate.

A narrow basket built around one idea.

From Maro’s belt hung something older.

A brass payment tag.

Three hundred yen owed to the boatwright who had repaired the mast of Maro’s abandoned vessel before his chase toward the oil storm.

The first payment had failed.

One retry remained.

Approval valid until moonrise.

Maro placed the tag beside the ticket.

— The basket gives me a path forward, — he said. — The payment closes what I left behind.

Tickoni looked at both marks.

— Separately, perhaps.

Before Maro could answer, fog rose from the sea.

Not drifting fog.

Advancing fog.

It moved against the wind in long white walls.

Bells rang inside it.

One bell.

Then ten.

Then hundreds beneath the water.

A black vessel emerged without sail, oar, or wake.

Pale sailors lined its rail. Their robes clung to them like wet burial cloth. Seaweed hung from their mouths. Small fish moved inside the hollows of their ribs.

Maro pulled his purse close.

— What are they?

Another ghost ship appeared behind the first.

Then another.

On the nearest deck, a drowned captain raised one white hand.

Maro reached toward the wooden scoop beside the water bucket.

A woman with coral woven through her hair leaned over the ghost ship’s rail.

Tickoni watched the water.

— The sea remembers unfinished things.

The fog beneath Maro’s boat brightened.

The four investments appeared as iron crates resting on the seabed.

The payment appeared as a smooth black stone chained to the stern.

Maro looked at Tickoni.

Knock.

Knock.

Dozens.

Hundreds.

# Release Notes

## Release: V1.3 — Portfolio And Cash Impact Loop

V1.3 completes the first combined consumer-money consequence loop: an
investment proposal and a payment proposal can now be evaluated together
against portfolio exposure, cash, buying power, pending obligations, approval
state, thesis drift, payment drift, and replay evidence.

```text
investment proposal + payment proposal -> combined impact model -> cards, alerts, suggestions, replay
```

What is now true: the user can inspect the weight of multiple proposed actions
before choosing what to carry. What is still not true: Tickoni does not
rebalance autonomously, retry payments autonomously, approve expired proposals,
place live trades, move money, call approved execution ledger, or bypass the policy and
proposal path. The release proves consequence visibility, not execution
authority.

## Headline Features

### 1. Combined Portfolio And Cash Impact

Tickoni now exposes one decision payload for the investment and payment paths
instead of treating them as unrelated outputs.

The payload shows:

* cash before and after,
* buying power before and after,
* pending obligations,
* asset-class exposure,
* sector exposure,
* ticker concentration,
* thesis exposure,
* rail and destination exposure,
* and display-ready explanations for material changes.

The important product change is that "allowed" is no longer the end of the
story. A trade can fit its ticket and a payment can fit its proposal, while
their combined cash impact still breaches the user’s buffer.

### 2. Thesis Cards And Money Proposal Cards

V1.3 persists the decision objects needed for a future UI to keep showing the
same money story after the initial interaction ends.

The release adds:

* thesis cards with thesis id, user text, basket id, linked positions, target
  exposure, current exposure, status, created time, and last checked time,
* money proposal cards with proposal id, source event, beneficiary, rail,
  currency, amount, approval state, expiry, evidence hash, and status,
* position and obligation links back to rationale and evidence,
* and a combined interface contract a CaseOps view can render later.

This turns the output from a chat-like answer into durable decision state:
what was proposed, why it existed, what it touched, and what evidence supports
it.

### 3. Drift, Approval, And Rebalance Signals

Tickoni now detects when the saved thesis or money proposal starts drifting.

The deterministic V1.3 path can surface:

* allocation, sector, concentration, instrument-eligibility, and buying-power
  drift,
* retry-window expiry, beneficiary-limit change, evidence expiry, approval
  expiry, approval revocation, and cash-buffer drift,
* a previewable rebalance suggestion,
* and a payment-proposal update.

Those outputs remain proposal-mode artifacts. They require user action and the
appropriate policy path before anything money-impacting happens.

### 4. General Instrument Classification Contract

V1.3 separates economic exposure from product type so policy and audit do not
have to infer meaning from tickers, labels, or themes.

The shared contract can represent:

* asset classes such as equity, fixed income, commodity, FX, crypto, and cash,
* instrument types such as stock, ETF, bond, option, future, fund, and token,
* sector and industry as separate taxonomy-backed dimensions,
* canonical theme identifiers as bounded lists,
* and catalog version, taxonomy id, taxonomy version, and code values for
  audit and replay.

Adding a new classification such as `chemicals`, `gold`, `solana`, or
`memecoins` now belongs in catalog or taxonomy data, not a new hardcoded field
shape.

## Governance and Safety

### 1. Consequence Is Visible Before Permission Becomes Action

V1.3 makes combined consequence a first-class contract. The user can see cash
reduction, exposure increase, narrowed buying power, pending obligations, and
cash-buffer breach before acting.

The release preserves the core boundary:

* the supplier payout remains approval-required and unexecuted,
* rebalance suggestions remain preview-only,
* payment proposal updates require user action,
* and expired approvals cannot move money.

### 2. Classification Screening Fails Closed

Intent filters, catalog lookup, basket screening, and policy checks now match
explicit classification dimensions instead of relying on one fixed
`sector_theme` selector.

Unsupported or malformed scope fails closed for:

* asset class,
* instrument type,
* market,
* venue,
* sector,
* industry,
* theme,
* and explicit instruments.

Every denial exposes the failed classification or scope dimension through the
interface contract and audit evidence.

### 3. Replay Carries Classification And Impact Evidence

Replay now has enough context to reproduce the combined decision without
reinterpreting labels or calling external systems.

The replay contract records the schema, catalog, taxonomy, and policy versions
used to produce the captured run. Drift in taxonomy, classification, catalog,
policy, normalized ordering, or unsupported schema versions becomes explicit
replay divergence or rejection.

Replay still never calls a live model, broker, payment API, ledger, financial
adapter, or privileged executor.

## Platform Work

### 1. UI-Ready Combined Decision Payload

V1.3 adds one combined runtime/interface payload for a future CaseOps client.
It includes portfolio impact, decision cards, drift contract, rebalance
suggestion, payment proposal update, replay summary, and no-execution state.

The payload is deterministic and fixture-backed, so product review and
engineering validation do not depend on live model output, broker state,
payment processor state, market data, approved execution ledger, or trading APIs.

### 2. Deterministic Portfolio/Cash System Proof

The release adds a dedicated V1.3 system proof for the combined portfolio and
cash demo. It validates that the runtime can produce before/after impact,
persist thesis and money proposal cards, trigger drift fixtures, emit a
previewable rebalance suggestion, and replay the result with external effects
disabled.

### 3. Shared Classification Schema And Fixtures

V1.3 moves instrument classification into shared schema, codec, catalog, and
fixture contracts. Existing V1.1 catalog fixtures preserve their allow, deny,
and restriction outcomes after the migration, while new fixtures prove broader
classification values can be represented without granting trading authority.
