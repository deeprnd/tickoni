Tickoni closes M0 in this release.

## Episode 02: Firedancer

The sea did not welcome Tickoni.

Without the Order’s seal, no gate opened at its approach. No ledger turned its pages. No marked current offered passage.

So Tickoni crossed the Ledger Sea the slow way, in a narrow black boat with a torn sail and a lantern that burned without oil.

Behind it stood the village with too many maps.

Ahead waited waters that did not care what anyone believed.

For one day, Tickoni sailed east.

By evening, the wind turned north.

By midnight, the current carried the boat west.

Tickoni adjusted the sail.

The sea adjusted first.

On the second night, clouds covered the stars. The lantern showed rope, wet wood, and Tickoni’s claws gripping the rail.

No path appeared beyond them.

The Council had taken the outer seal.

Something still burned inside Tickoni.

Something chosen.

But choice did not fill a sail.

Choice did not steer a boat.

Near midnight, the sea became still.

Not calm.

Still.

The waves flattened into black glass. The sail hung loose. Even the lantern flame stood straight.

Far ahead, a shore appeared.

A thin line of black sand curved beneath the moon. One cedar tree stood on it. Beside the tree burned a small red lamp.

Land.

Rest.

A place to fix the sail.

Tickoni took the oar and turned toward it.

The boat moved.

By dawn, the shore remained ahead.

No closer.

The water behind showed no wake.

The boat had moved all night.

The sea had remembered nothing.

Tickoni turned again.

The shore shifted east.

Tickoni turned east.

The shore shifted north.

Tickoni turned north.

For hours, Tickoni followed.

Each correction seemed right.

Each correction came too late.

No monster attacked. No wave crushed the hull. No dragon opened its eye beneath the boat.

That made it worse.

There was no enemy to strike.

Only a shore that stayed almost reachable.

By the third evening, the sail had torn wider. Salt dried white along the rail. Tickoni’s arms ached from rowing toward land that did not arrive.

The red lamp glowed brighter.

Tickoni lowered the oar.

The boat drifted.

The shore drifted with it.

Tickoni placed one claw over the fire beneath its ribs.

— I chose.

The sea remained still.

— I chose.

No current came.

No path answered.

Then something tapped the water.

Tap.

A spark appeared far to the west.

Tap.

Another spark.

Tap.

A third.

Not lightning.

Not lanterns.

Footsteps.

No.

Not footsteps.

A dance.

A figure in black cloth spun across the Ledger Sea, one heel touching a wave crest, one toe sliding along the rib of a drowned ship. He kicked off a reef tooth, caught a floating chain, turned once in the air, and landed on a strip of foam as if the sea had been waiting for him.

Each step flashed red.

Each step vanished.

The figure moved like fire given ankles and too much confidence.

He crossed in breaths what Tickoni had spent three nights failing to cross.

Then he reached the black shore.

The red lamp flickered.

The cedar tree bent.

The shore slid north.

The figure did not follow.

He stopped where the shore had been and turned slowly on one heel.

The black sand thinned into mist. The cedar tree folded like smoke. The red lamp became a moon reflection trembling on the water.

Then even that disappeared.

Tickoni stared.

The figure had not been chasing the shore.

Five sparks later, he stood beside the boat.

The sea hissed beneath his feet.

— Are you one of the Order? — Tickoni asked.

The figure laughed.

Not politely.

He laughed so hard one heel slipped into the water.

He pulled it back at once and glared down, as if the sea had insulted him.

— The Order? No. The Order marches.

He bowed with one arm across his chest and one arm raised like flame caught in wind.

— I dance.

Tickoni studied the last footprint. It burned for one breath longer than the others, then vanished without smoke.

— Then who are you?

The figure straightened.

— Firedancer, keeper of the fast path, breaker of bottlenecks, prince of the skipped queue, validator of suspicious puddles, terror of slow bridges, and temporary master of wherever my feet have not yet exploded.

He waited.

Tickoni said nothing.

Firedancer sighed.

— Usually there is more reaction.

— Why did you stop here?

Firedancer sat on the boat’s rail. The wood did not burn.

From somewhere inside his sleeve, he produced a pickled agave fruit, bit into it, and looked at the torn sail.

Then the oar.

Then the empty place where the shore had been.

— Curiosity.

— About what?

— Whether you were lost professionally.

Tickoni frowned.

— I am not lost.

Firedancer looked around the empty sea.

— A bold local custom.

Tickoni gripped the rail.

— I am crossing the Ledger Sea.

— To where?

Tickoni looked east.

No gate waited there. No old path answered. The black sparks had vanished beyond the horizon.

But the vow remained.

— I going to forge a contract no oni can break.

Firedancer stopped chewing.

Then he spat the agave into the sea.

It hissed.

— You?

Tickoni’s ears lowered.

Firedancer looked at the boat.

Then at Tickoni.

Then at the boat again.

— You cannot forge a path through a puddle.

— This is not a puddle.

— That makes it even worse!

Firedancer hopped backward onto the water.

Five ember footprints appeared across the sea.

The first rested on a ripple.

The second on a floating reed.

The third on a dark stone just beneath the surface.

The fourth on the back of a passing fish.

The fifth on nothing Tickoni could see.

Firedancer stretched his arms.

— One cannot forge an unbreakable contract with feet like dumped cargo.

He cracked his neck.

— Also, I am bored.

Tickoni stared at the marks.

— That is not a path.

— It is five steps.

— The fifth is empty.

— Then watch better.

Firedancer crossed them again.

Ripple.

Reed.

Stone.

Fish.

Nothing.

He moved lightly, almost lazily, one hand behind his back.

— Your turn.

Tickoni stepped onto the rail.

No gate opened.

No old current rose.

The first footprint burned.

Tickoni stepped.

Its foot punched straight through the ripple.

Cold water swallowed its leg.

Firedancer caught its wrist before the sea took the rest.

For a moment, Tickoni hung upside down beside the boat, dripping.

Firedancer looked at it.

— Heavy.

— I am not heavy.

— Your foot is.

Tickoni pulled itself back onto the rail.

— I stepped where you stepped.

— You stepped where I had been.

Firedancer tapped the ripple with one toe.

Two small waves touched, lifted each other for less than a breath, then passed apart.

— This held me when the waves met. Then it stopped being a step.

He pointed to the reed.

— This holds only while it floats between currents.

The reed spun away.

He pointed to the stone.

— This holds only where the pull is weak.

The water darkened around it.

He pointed to the fish.

— This holds only if you wait.

The fish surfaced, blinked, and vanished.

Firedancer pointed to the fifth mark.

— And this?

Tickoni saw nothing.

Firedancer smiled.

— Warm water. Cold water. They meet here.

He placed one foot on empty sea.

The water held.

Tickoni narrowed its eyes.

— That is cheating.

— That is looking.

Again, Firedancer made the five steps.

This time he added a clap.

Clap.

Ripple.

Clap.

Reed.

Clap.

Stone.

Clap.

Fish.

Clap.

Seam.

The rhythm hung over the water.

— Receive.

Clap.

— Carry.

Clap.

— Check.

Clap.

— Record.

Clap.

— Stop.

Clap.

Tickoni blinked.

— Are those rules?

— They are steps.

— They sound like rules.

— Good steps usually do.

Tickoni tried again.

First step.

The water held.

Second step.

The reed dipped.

Tickoni waved both arms.

Firedancer clapped once.

— Less statue.

Tickoni bent its knees.

The reed held.

Third step.

The stone cut its foot.

Tickoni hissed.

— The stone bit me.

— The stone has standards.

Fourth step.

The fish surfaced.

Tickoni hesitated.

The fish vanished.

Tickoni fell.

Firedancer caught it again.

— Too slow? — Tickoni asked.

— Too much dignity. The fish is not judging you.

One more time.

Ripple.

Reed.

Stone.

Fish.

Seam.

For one breath, Tickoni stood on the fifth step.

The sea held.

The boat waited behind it.

The open water waited ahead.

Beneath Tickoni’s chest, the chosen fire answered the ember marks.

Not with a name.

Only heat.

Firedancer nodded.

— There.

Tickoni looked down.

— I am standing on nothing.

— No. You are standing on what you finally noticed.

The seam moved.

Tickoni jumped back to the boat before it fell.

Almost gracefully.

Not quite.

Firedancer allowed this to count.

Dawn paled the edge of the Ledger Sea. Black reefs rose ahead, sharp as broken teeth.

Firedancer pointed east.

— Your boat can pass there.

Tickoni looked at the reefs.

— The gap is too narrow.

— Then stop staring at the gap.

— What should I watch?

Firedancer tapped the boat’s rail.

— What touches the boat.

A small wave struck the hull.

— What carries it.

The boat tilted with the current.

— What tests the way.

Foam covered the third black tooth, then cleared.

— What mark remains.

Tickoni scratched a line into the rail.

— And when the sea lies.

The foam returned.

Tickoni waited.

The foam cleared again.

A narrow channel opened.

Tickoni adjusted the sail.

Not toward the vanished shore.

Toward the seam between wind and current.

The boat turned.

Slowly.

Correctly.

The first reef passed to port.

The second passed to starboard.

The third vanished under foam.

Tickoni waited.

The sea opened.

The boat slipped through.

Behind it, the wake finally remained.

A thin black line held on the water where the boat had crossed.

Not long.

Long enough.

Ahead, Firedancer stood on the last reef, wrapped in ember-red light.

— Better.

Tickoni looked at the wake.

— Is this your path?

— No.

— Mine?

— For now.

— Where does it lead?

Firedancer smiled.

— Better question next time.

Then he spun east.

Three red turns.

One kick from the foam.

Gone.

Tickoni stood alone in the boat.

The Ledger Sea moved around it.

Still vast.

Still indifferent.

But no longer blank.

Beneath the wound where the seal had been, five narrow lines warmed once and faded before Tickoni could name them.

Tickoni looked at the rail.

At the water behind the boat.

For a moment, the sea remembered where it had crossed.

Not long.

Long enough.

Tickoni raised the torn sail.

This time, the boat did not chase the horizon.

It followed the water beneath it.

# Release Notes

## Release: [M0: The Trust Foundation](https://github.com/deeprnd/tickoni/milestone/13)

M0 closes in this release.

Its two epics now ship together:

* [V0.0: Proof Of Correctness](../epics/v0.0.md)
* [V0.37: Firedancer Integration](../epics/v0.37.md)

V0.0 established the deterministic event path: synthetic financial events can be framed, normalized, deduplicated, policy-checked, audited, replayed, and inspected without external effects.

V0.37 hardens the boundary around that proof: Tickoni now builds and tests against the Firedancer substrate it actually reuses, exposes Tickoni-owned product topologies, and keeps Solana validator concepts out of the Tickoni runtime surface.

```text
V0.0: prove the deterministic event path
-> V0.37: harden the Firedancer boundary around it
-> M0: trust foundation closed
```

The operator now has a product-owned runtime foundation instead of a proof sitting too close to borrowed validator structure. This release still does not run agents, call models, connect to brokers, databases, or payment networks, move money, or place trades. It closes the trust foundation milestone those later flows must stand on.

## Headline Features

### 1. M0 Closes With Exactly Two Epics

M0 now has its full intended shape:

* `V0.0` delivered the deterministic runtime proof,
* and `V0.37` delivered the Firedancer integration boundary that makes that proof product-owned.

There are no additional hidden M0 epics. The milestone closes here.

### 2. Standalone Firedancer Substrate Build

Tickoni now has a cleaner build contract with its reused C substrate.

`just build-fd` builds the Firedancer libraries Tickoni actually reuses:

* `fd_tango`,
* `fd_util`,
* `fd_ballet`,
* `fd_disco`,
* and `fd_waltz`.

That build path excludes the validator product surface: Solana validator tiles, RPC schemas, unrelated binaries, and other non-Tickoni artifacts do not define the Tickoni build story.

`just build-tk` then builds the Zig `tickoni-supervisor` against that substrate through Tickoni-owned wrappers.

### 3. Tickoni-Owned Product Topology

Tickoni's product topology is now explicit about being Tickoni's.

The product runtime registers Tickoni tile IDs such as:

* `tkings`,
* `tknorm`,
* `tkdedu`,
* `tkpoly`,
* `tkaudt`,
* `tkrepl`,
* `tkmetr`,
* and `tkdiag`.

The declared investment workflow likewise uses Tickoni tile IDs such as `tkcase`, `tkdisp`, `tkagnt`, `tkmodl`, `tktool`, and `tkadpt`.

Validator tile identities are no longer the conceptual frame for the product runtime. Firedancer remains the infrastructure substrate, not the product vocabulary.

### 4. Boundary Verification In Build And Test

V0.37 adds evidence that the Firedancer boundary is real and maintained, not just described in docs.

The repository now has build and test lanes that verify the integration surface:

* `just build-fd`,
* `just build-tk`,
* `just test-unit-tk`,
* and `just test-integration-tk`.

Those lanes exercise the product topology, Firedancer-backed link and workspace plumbing, and Tickoni-owned runtime wrappers without making Solana validator semantics part of the product contract.

### 5. Documentation And Boundary Clarity

The architecture, topology, build, and testing docs now tell the same story:

* Firedancer is the systems substrate,
* Tickoni is the product runtime,
* Solana validator semantics are excluded from Tickoni's product surface,
* and the active Tickoni runtime lives under `src/app/tickoni/` and `src/tickoni/`.

This release is as much about precision of identity as about build output.

## Governance and Safety

### 1. The Runtime Boundary Is Explicit

Tickoni now draws a clearer line between inherited substrate and owned product behavior.

Firedancer contributes queues, topology discipline, workspaces, lifecycle, metrics, diagnostics, and related systems primitives. Tickoni owns the product topology, tile identities, financial semantics, and runtime surface.

That separation reduces ambiguity for operators, reviewers, and later feature work.

### 2. Product Topology Does Not Inherit Validator Semantics

This release keeps Solana validator concepts out of Tickoni's product runtime shape.

The product topology is described in Tickoni tile IDs and Tickoni docs. Validator tile names and validator-only flows are not presented as the product runtime.

### 3. External Effects Still Stay Disabled

Closing M0 does not change the no-side-effects rule established by V0.0.

There is still no live model provider call, broker call, payment call, approved execution ledger mutation, trade placement, or money movement in this release.

V0.37 hardens the foundation. It does not widen authority.

### 4. Fail-Closed Integration Matters

Build isolation and topology clarity are not cosmetic.

They reduce the chance that Tickoni accidentally inherits unrelated protocol assumptions, toolchain requirements, or product semantics from the validator codebase it borrows infrastructure from.

## Platform Work

### 1. `tickoni_fd` Build Scope

The Firedancer-side build is now explicitly scoped to what Tickoni reuses.

That scope narrows the C dependency surface and gives CI and developers a repo-facing contract that matches the product boundary.

### 2. Separate Build Recipes For Substrate And Product

The repo-facing command surface now clearly separates:

* `just build-fd` for the reused Firedancer substrate,
* and `just build-tk` for the Tickoni runtime.

That makes it easier to reason about which layer changed, failed, or needs review.

### 3. Topology Definitions Live In Tickoni-Owned Code

The active product topologies are declared in `src/app/tickoni/topologies.zig`, not inferred from validator registration tables.

That keeps product identity and runtime wiring in the part of the tree Tickoni owns.

## Demo

Run the M0 closure proof with:

```bash
just build-fd
just build-tk
just test-unit-tk
just test-integration-tk
```

The commands should prove that Tickoni's runtime foundation now has both halves of M0:

1. the deterministic Tickoni runtime proof from `V0.0`,
2. and the cleaned Firedancer integration boundary from `V0.37`.

To inspect the active Tickoni topology surface:

```bash
zig build run -- status
```
