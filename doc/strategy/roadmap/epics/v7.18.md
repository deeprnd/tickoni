# V7.18: The Self-Verifying Replay

**Status: Planned · Milestone: M7**

**Epic:** #TBD · **Issue:** #TBD
**Labels:** `type/epic`, `area/audit`, `area/platform`, `area/trust`

When V1.11 established replay, it proved the system could reconstruct an investment flow from captured inputs and fail closed on any divergence. But replay was a black box: auditors and partners could not independently verify the result without re-running the flow, and the evidence trail carried no cryptographic anchors that proved it hadn't been altered. V7.18 turns the replay capsule into a self-verifying proof bundle — content-addressed evidence, hash-chained event references, and expected-outcome verification — so that anyone with the capsule and the evidence can confirm the replay happened exactly as it was recorded. Not because we trust the system, but because anyone else needs to be able to.

The logical fix for "auditors can't verify replay results" is to add more validation tools, more export formats, more manual review steps. The psychological fix is to make the evidence *cryptographically self-verifying* — every piece content-addressed, every sequence hash-chained, every outcome verifiable against an expected result. A tampered capsule doesn't just fail; it reports the exact sequence number and field where divergence occurred. In audit, that's not a side effect. That's the product.

**The infinite-game lens:** V1.11 proved we could replay. V7.18 proves we can *prove the replay* — to auditors, to partners, to anyone who needs to verify without re-running. The infinite game is about building systems that earn the right to be audited, not systems that pass audits by luck.

**The psycho-logic lens:** The obvious answer to "how do auditors verify replay?" is "let them re-run it." The counterintuitive insight is that *re-running is the failure mode*. A system that requires reproduction to verify is a system that doesn't trust itself. A replay capsule that verifies itself with content hashes and chain integrity is a system that says: "here's the proof, check it yourself, in seconds." That feeling — of verifiability without reproduction — is what separates a forensic replay tool from a generic audit log.

## Product Intent

V1.11 replay reconstructs the investment flow from captured inputs, routes model calls through `tkmodl` substitution, and fails closed on missing substitutions. What it does not provide is a tamper-evident proof bundle: evidence items are not content-addressed, the replay capsule does not carry hash references for events, transcript, or tool calls, and there is no expected outcome field to verify replay results against. V7.18 adds this integrity layer so that the capsule is self-verifying and the proof bundle can be shared with auditors and partners without re-running the flow.

**The product promise: a replay capsule that verifies itself.** An auditor with the capsule and the evidence can confirm the replay happened exactly as recorded — or detect tamper at the exact sequence number — without executing a single step. Content-addressed evidence means identical content always produces the same hash, regardless of capture time. Hash-chained event references mean tampering cannot be hidden by reordering or dropping events. Expected outcome verification means the system checks its own work before declaring success.

This epic does not change replay's substitution logic, fail-closed behavior, model gateway, or tool broker. It adds an integrity layer on top: content hashes, chain references, and outcome verification that make the capsule self-auditable.

---

## Users And Jobs

- [Auditor / partner]: Verify a replay proof bundle independently from content hashes and capsule references alone, detecting any tampering at the exact sequence number and field where it occurred — without re-running the flow.
- [Compliance operator]: Accept a replay capsule and its evidence as a single auditable unit — hash-stable, chain-verified, outcome-confirmed — and share it externally without losing trust or adding annotations.
- [System operator]: Know that a tampered capsule fails immediately at load (non-monotonic sequence rejection) and reports the first divergence with precision, rather than producing a misleading clean replay.
- [Risk reviewer]: Confirm that outcome mismatch — a reconstructed result that differs from the expected outcome — triggers a fail-closed state with a recorded divergence, not a silent pass.

---

## Success Metrics

- Every evidence item is content-addressed: identical content produces the same hash regardless of capture time or context
- A replay capsule with a non-monotonic event-hash sequence is rejected at load, before any replay step executes
- A tampered captured fixture produces a first-divergence record at the exact sequence number with the first differing field identified
- A reconstructed outcome that differs from the expected outcome field causes replay to fail closed with a divergence record — no silent acceptance
- An unmodified capsule replays cleanly, producing a matching outcome with zero divergence records
- Every tamper event, sequence rejection, and outcome mismatch is recorded as a tamper-detection audit entry with sequence number, field, and hash comparison

## Scope

### In Scope

- Content-addressed evidence store: stable canonical content hash, item id, source identity, sequence number, and verification endpoints
- Hash-chained event references in the replay capsule: ordered event hashes with monotonicity enforcement at load time
- Agent transcript hash and tool-call hash references stored within the capsule
- Expected outcome field for replay verification, populated at capture and validated at replay
- First-divergence records on tamper detection and outcome mismatch, with sequence number, field, and hash delta
- Integration tests and deterministic fixtures for hash stability, sequence validation, tamper detection, and outcome mismatch
- Audit JSONL samples for clean replay, tamper detection, sequence rejection, and outcome mismatch

### Out Of Scope

- No changes to replay substitution logic or fail-closed behavior (owned by V1.11)
- No changes to `tkmodl` or `tktool` dispatch
- No new product capabilities or policy rules
- No changes to the audit record schema outside the replay capsule
- No CaseOps API or UI changes — proof bundles are queryable via existing paths
- No live model, broker, payment, trading, crypto, or approved execution side effects

### How Many Stories

Two stories: S1 owns the integrity mechanism (content-addressed evidence, hash-chained capsule, outcome verification, first-divergence records), S2 owns the evidence layer (deterministic tests, fixtures, demo closure, quality gates). This is a tightly scoped epic — two stories is correct.

### Decomposition Rules

1. **Independent delivery**: S1 delivers the integrity layer (content hashes + chain references + outcome verification); S2 delivers verification (tests + fixtures + demo closure)
2. **One capability per story**: S1 = integrity enforcement, S2 = evidence and release — no mixing
3. **Closure story**: S2 handles evidence, demo, documentation, and quality gates via its child tasks (T10)
4. **Domain boundaries**: integrity enforcement and evidence are cleanly separated

### Bad Splits (Avoid)

- Splitting content-addressed evidence, hash-chained capsules, and outcome verification into three separate stories — they are facets of the same integrity mechanism.
- Making audit records a separate story from the hashes that trigger them — the audit record is the integrity output, not a separate capability.
- Splitting integration tests from the feature they verify — deterministic tests belong with the story they validate.
- Isolating the expected outcome field from first-divergence records — they are two sides of the same verification mechanism.
