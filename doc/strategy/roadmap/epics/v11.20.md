# V11.20: Policy-Gated Crypto Transfers

**Status:** Backlog · **Milestone:** M11 · **Labels:** `epic`, `area/crypto`, `area/security`, `area/audit`, `area/trust`

V11.20 proves that moving crypto money out of Tickoni requires the same
capability-checked governance as everything else — the capability envelope,
the approval gate, the signed action, the read-back. A spot trade that
creates a spendable balance does not grant transfer authority, and every
wallet, network, fee, compliance, and chain-risk check is visible to the
operator before a single unit can leave custody.

## Product Intent

In consumer finance, people need to move crypto and stablecoins — to a wallet,
an exchange, or a settlement rail. But the instinct that "if I can trade it, I
can move it" is the gap this epic closes. V11.20 makes that gap visible:
trading authority and transfer authority are separate, and the operator sees
every policy check, compliance gate, and replay proof before any transfer
intent can proceed.

The product promise is simple: **asking to move crypto triggers a transparent,
policy-checked proposal with wallet, network, fee, compliance, and no-bypass
evidence — and nothing moves until a human approves the exact proposal hash.**

## Users And Jobs

- Crypto or stablecoin user: request a transfer or withdrawal and see whether
  the wallet, network, asset, amount, fee, and evidence are allowed before any
  money can move.
- CaseOps operator: review a transfer proposal, blocked reasons, approval
  state, signed envelope, read-back status, audit timeline, and replay result.
- Compliance or risk reviewer: inspect chain-risk, travel-rule, wallet
  allowlist, counterparty, source-of-funds, and jurisdiction evidence before
  approving or denying the transfer.
- Runtime owner: verify that adapters, execution, audit, replay, metrics, and
  diagnostics prove no direct wallet, exchange, bridge, or custody bypass.

## Success Metrics

- A deterministic local flow turns a custody or post-trade balance into a
  transfer proposal with source account, asset, network, destination wallet,
  amount, fee estimate, risk evidence, policy outcome, and proposal hash.
- Unsupported networks, unsupported assets, wallet mismatches, stale fee
  estimates, missing travel-rule evidence, high chain-risk, excessive amount,
  duplicate action id, expired approval, and direct adapter bypass fail closed.
- Trading capabilities from V6.9 cannot satisfy `crypto_transfer.propose`,
  `crypto_transfer.prepare`, or `crypto_transfer.initiate`.
- Paper mode remains the default. Sandbox transfer submission requires an
  explicitly enabled signed adapter manifest, policy allow or required approval,
  exact proposal hash binding, deterministic action id, and read-back
  reconciliation.
- Replay reproduces transfer proposal, policy outcome, approval state,
  submission substitute, read-back result, and final state without live wallet,
  chain, exchange, bridge, model, adapter, or execution side effects.

## Demo Moment

- Command or flow: a deterministic local CaseOps or CLI flow for a USDC
  transfer from an approved custody account to an allowlisted wallet on a
  sandbox network fixture, plus blocked wallet, blocked network, stale-fee,
  and missing-travel-rule scenarios.
- Expected result: the operator sees an allowed or approval-required transfer
  proposal, a paper or sandbox submission state when explicitly enabled, exact
  fee and destination evidence, blocked examples, append-only audit output, and
  replay match with external effects disabled.

## Scope

### In Scope

- Crypto transfer intent schema for withdrawals, internal custody moves,
  exchange-to-wallet moves, wallet-to-custody deposit reconciliation, and
  stablecoin cash movement.
- Candidate network decision record for one initial popular L1 or L2 sandbox
  rail, with explicit unresolved dimensions before implementation.
- Source custody or exchange account snapshot, spendable balance, locked
  balance, pending transfer, and post-transfer balance representation.
- Destination wallet allowlist, network allowlist, asset allowlist,
  counterparty allowlist, memo/tag/payment-id handling where applicable, and
  address-format validation fixtures.
- Fee and gas estimate capture with freshness, max fee, native gas balance,
  fee payer, and final amount checks.
- Chain-risk, sanctions, travel-rule, jurisdiction, and source-of-funds
  evidence gates where configured by policy.
- `crypto_transfer.propose`, `crypto_transfer.prepare`, and approved sandbox
  `crypto_transfer.initiate` capability checks with explicit policy outcomes.
- Adapter reads and sandbox submission through `tktool`, `tkadpt`, and future
  approved `tkexec` boundaries only; no direct agent or UI wallet access.
- Append-only audit, evidence, metrics, diagnostics, and replay samples for
  allowed, approval-required, blocked, tampered, and bypass-attempt flows.

### Out Of Scope

- No production live crypto transfers by default.
- No autonomous transfer execution, withdrawal approval, custody policy
  override, or wallet key access for agents or UI.
- No bridge transfers, DeFi swaps, staking, lending, yield strategies, cross-
  chain routing, token wrapping, or contract interaction beyond simple transfer
  fixtures.
- No final blockchain, wallet provider, custody provider, exchange, bridge, or
  L2 selection without an explicit product and architecture decision.
- No direct approved execution ledger, analytics store, Markdown, model-provider, wallet, chain RPC,
  exchange, custody, or bridge access from agents or CaseOps UI.
- No reuse of Solana validator schemas, RPC semantics, or validator tile
  identities as Tickoni crypto transfer semantics.

## Story Breakdown

### How Many Stories?

Six stories — the standard mid-to-large range (4-7+) for an epic that spans
core capability (S1-S3), policy enforcement (S4), lifecycle (S5), and
evidence/demo/closure (S6). Each story delivers an independently observable
outcome per the decomposition rules.

### Decomposition Rules

1. **Independent delivery**: each story must produce a user-visible or
   operator-visible outcome without waiting for every other story.
2. **One capability per story**: do not split CRUD from validation. If
   creating an entity requires validation (rejecting negative values, checking
   required fields, enforcing bounds), that validation belongs in the same
   story. Validation is part of the CRUD contract, not a separate story.
3. **Split by capability lifecycle**, not by layer:
   - S1: Core capability — the primary create/edit/delete or action flow
     with its validation, capability envelope, and audit trail.
   - S2: Read / query / visibility — the operator-facing surface (CaseOps,
     API, terminal) that surfaces the capability's state.
   - S3: State management — lifecycle transitions (activate, suspend, delete,
     migrate) that depend on S1 core being stable.
   - S4: Policy integration — capability-specific policy checks, approval
     paths, and scope enforcement via `tkpoly`.
   - S5: Replay and audit — deterministic replay support, replay capsules,
     hash-chained audit records for the capability's material events.
   - S6+: Domain-specific additions — model calls, adapter dispatch, storage,
     observability, or integration points the epic touches.
4. **Closure story**: the last story (or the last task in the final story, per
   the story template's T10 child task) handles evidence, demo, documentation,
   and quality gates. Do not duplicate this as a separate story line when the
   story template already covers it via child tasks.
5. **Domain boundaries**: if the epic touches multiple runtime boundaries
   (tile ownership, model gateway, adapter, UI), those are separate stories,
   not features within one story.

### Bad Splits (Avoid)

- CRUD + "validation" as two stories. Validation is part of the CRUD contract.
- "Backend" + "frontend" as two stories. Both should be independently testable
  outcomes, not layers of the same feature.
- "Database" + "API" as two stories. Same reason.
- Anything that requires ALL other stories to complete before one acceptance
  criterion can be tested. That is not independent delivery.

### Good Split Example (Portfolio Management Epic)

```
V5.1.S1: Portfolio CRUD — create/edit/delete portfolios and holdings with
         validation, capability envelopes, and audit trail.
V5.1.S2: Portfolio CaseOps surface — portfolio state and mutations visible
         through CaseOps with stable event IDs and hashes.
V5.1.S3: Portfolio replay — deterministic replay of portfolio mutations
         against the same event stream with stable hashes and reconstructed state.
V5.1.S4: Evidence, demo, and release closure — T10 expansion covering
         fixtures, quality gates, documentation, and roadmap updates.
```

Note: S4 is the closure, implemented as child tasks (T10) within that story
per the story template convention, not as a standalone epic-level story.

## Release / Evidence Gate

- What can the user or operator do now that they could not before this epic?
  Create, review, approve, paper-run, or explicitly sandbox-submit a guarded
  crypto or stablecoin transfer with visible wallet, network, fee, compliance,
  and replay evidence — without moving money until a human approves the exact
  proposal hash.
- **What changed:** V6.9 proved guarded spot trading and kept trading authority
  separate from all other authority. V11.20 extends that separation to money
  movement, adding wallet, network, fee, travel-rule, and chain-risk gates
  while keeping transfer authority strictly separate from trade authority.
- What is this epic's wow-effect: the visible moment in this epic's demo or
  workflow that makes Tickoni feel unlike a generic agent harness?
  An operator asks to move USDC and Tickoni shows the exact destination wallet,
  network, fee estimate, compliance checks, approval state, and no-bypass proof
  — every gate visible before a single unit can leave custody. The constraint
  is the trust signal.
- **Unique value:** high-throughput event processing, consequence isolation,
  policy-gated proposals, and forensic replay applied to crypto money
  movement — not just trading, not just payments, but a unified safety model
  across all crypto outflows.
- **Demo command or CaseOps flow:** deterministic local CaseOps or CLI flow for
  an approved stablecoin or crypto transfer plus blocked wallet, blocked
  network, stale-fee, and missing-travel-rule scenarios.
- **Checks enforced:** source account, custody provider, wallet, network, asset,
  token contract where applicable, counterparty, amount, fee, native gas
  balance, transfer frequency, chain-risk, travel-rule, jurisdiction, evidence,
  approval, and adapter manifest checks.
- **What happens when the requested action exceeds policy, scope, or evidence:**
  `tkpoly` returns `deny`, `require_approval`, `require_more_evidence`, or
  `escalate`; no proposal preparation or submission occurs beyond the allowed
  state.
- **Execution mode:** paper is default. Sandbox transfer submission is allowed
  only through an explicitly enabled signed adapter manifest and approval path.
  Live execution is out of scope by default.
- **Proving artifacts:** intent, custody account, wallet allowlist, network, fee
  estimate, chain-risk, travel-rule, policy, approval, adapter substitute,
  read-back, audit JSONL, replay capsule, and blocked-flow fixtures.
- **Replay without side effects:** yes — replay substitutes model, wallet,
  chain, custody, exchange, bridge, adapter, execution, and read-back outputs.
- **Fail-closed proof:** wallet-not-allowlisted, stale-fee, unsupported-network,
  missing-travel-rule, and tampered proposal-hash fixtures all fail closed with
  a policy denial and audit record before any adapter call.

## Dependencies And Decisions

- Depends on V1.5 crypto/stablecoin guard policy dimensions.
- Builds on V1.6 signed adapter manifest, action id, idempotency, read-back,
  and kill-switch patterns when sandbox submission is enabled.
- Builds on V6.9 crypto spot trading output while keeping trading and transfer
  authority separate.
- Decision needed: initial popular L1 or L2 sandbox network, wallet/address
  format, token-contract scope, fee asset, finality assumptions, chain-risk
  provider fixture, travel-rule fixture, adapter manifest, and read-back shape.
- Decision needed: whether approved sandbox transfer submission is owned by
  existing adapter/execution boundaries or requires a new tile/topology change.
