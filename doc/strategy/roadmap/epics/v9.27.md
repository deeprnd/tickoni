# V9.27: External Research Signal Pipeline

**Status:** Backlog
**Milestone:** M9
**Labels:** `epic`, `area/audit`, `area/investing`, `area/platform`, `area/security`, `type/documentation`

## Why

Information without provenance is just opinion. In financial markets, the ideas that move portfolios start as someone's interpretation of a market event — an earnings call, a macro shift, a technical breakdown — and the value of that interpretation collapses the moment the chain of custody is broken.

An investment analyst watches a YouTube analysis. They form a view. They recommend a position. The chain is: person → video → interpretation → recommendation. No audit system can verify any link in that chain. Not because it's hard, but because nothing is recorded. The analyst's judgment is real, the video's commentary is real, but the *connection between them* vanishes into memory the moment the screen goes dark.

Generic research tools solve the *convenience* problem: "here's a feed, here's a summary." They cannot solve the *credibility* problem: "how do I prove, to compliance, to my team, to myself, that this recommendation is grounded in what I actually watched?" The engineering answer would be "build a better data pipeline." But the actual problem is trust — and trust isn't built by speed. It's built by the willingness to make yourself traceable.

This epic is about creating that traceability. Every research source — not just the internal ones we already govern, but the external ones the analyst chooses to follow — is folded into the same governed pipeline that enforces capability, audit, replay, and approval. The result is not a research tool. It is proof that research exists.

## What (in one paragraph)

Tickoni's first external-source research signal pipeline: a governed YouTube monitoring and summarization system that reads only the authenticated user's subscribed channels, detects new videos, fetches metadata and captions, generates source-grounded summaries through `tkmodl`, and surfaces those summaries as audit-chained evidence in CaseOps. The analyst selects their own channels — the system monitors what they chose, summarizes what they should see, and every summary is hash-chained, replayable, and policy-gated. No transcript storage. No model training. No trade recommendations. No auto-execution. Just a complete, verifiable chain from video to evidence.

## The Psychological Insight

The logical approach to research ingestion is: "get more data faster." Every competitor's approach is the same — scrape everything, summarize everything, drown the analyst in noise and hope the signal emerges. The psycho-logic approach is the inverse: *the bottleneck isn't information volume; it's provenance density.*

An analyst already has access to thousands of financial analyses on YouTube. What they don't have is a way to say "here is the exact claim, the exact timestamp, the exact video, that led me to this recommendation." That's not a technology gap — it's a *framing* gap. If every research input arrives inside the same governed pipeline as internal events, with the same hash chains, audit records, and replay capability, the analyst's external research *becomes* internal evidence by default. No extra tool. No extra process. The structure does the work.

The costly signal here is replay. A generic scraper cannot replay. A Google Sheet cannot replay. An email forward cannot replay. But a hash-chained, summary-substituted, fully-deterministic replay capsule is something only a system willing to commit its infrastructure can produce. That willingness is the signal.

## Circle of Safety

This pipeline doesn't constrain the analyst. It *empowers* them by making their work auditable. When every research input is traceable, the analyst doesn't need to defend their sources — the audit trail does it for them. When every summary is policy-gated, they don't need to worry about unauthorized claims slipping in — the capability envelope catches it. When replay is deterministic, compliance doesn't need to ask "what happened?" — the replay shows them.

That is the Circle of Safety. The analyst operates within boundaries that are explicit, consistent, and enforced by the system, not by politics. They know exactly what's allowed (their own subscriptions, caption-based summaries, policy-gated signals) and exactly what's not (bulk scraping, transcript storage, unauthorized channels). Inside those boundaries, they are free.

## Users And Jobs

- **Investment analyst / operator:** Selects which subscribed YouTube channels to monitor. Receives structured summaries with source links and timestamps. Views summaries as audit-chained evidence alongside trading proposals in CaseOps. The job is not "consume more research" — it is "consume research I can prove I used."

- **Compliance / risk operator:** Verifies which external research sources informed each proposal. Traces any claim to a timestamped video segment. Confirms no unauthorized channels were monitored and no transcripts were stored. The job is not "police the analyst" — it is "verify the chain."

- **Consumer user:** Stays informed about financial markets through YouTube channels they follow, with clear boundaries on monitoring scope, content handling, and output framing.

## Success Metrics

- A user can authenticate a YouTube account, select subscribed channels, and trigger a full ingest cycle from video detection to CaseOps evidence without any hardcoded credentials or bulk-scraping paths.
- Every ingest event produces a hash-chained audit record with video URL, summary hash, timestamp references, and source attribution.
- Replay of any ingest event produces the identical summary and audit record without calling the YouTube API.
- All model calls for summary generation route through `tkmodl` with token budgets and context limits enforced.
- All YouTube Data API calls route through `tkadpt` as a governed adapter path.

## Demo Moment

Authenticate YouTube account → select 3 subscribed channels → trigger manual poll → wait for new-video detection → view summary in CaseOps → replay the ingest event from fixture → verify identical summary and audit record.

Expected result: a source-grounded summary with video URL, timestamp references, and "not investment advice" framing appears in CaseOps; replay produces the identical output without external API calls.

## Scope

### In Scope

- OAuth authentication via Google/YouTube account (OAuth-scoped only)
- Reading user's subscribed channels via YouTube Data API
- User selection of which subscribed channels to monitor
- Periodic detection of new videos on monitored channels
- Fetching video metadata: title, URL, thumbnail, publish date, description
- Caption/subtitle download (only when available via official API)
- Summary generation via `tkmodl` with metadata-only fallback and disclaimer
- Capability envelope definition: `research_signal.youtube_ingest` in `tkpoly`
- Audit recording of ingestion events in `tkaudt`
- Evidence attachment in `tkevid` when available
- CaseOps surface for viewing YouTube-sourced research evidence
- Timestamp references alongside summaries
- Source-linking back to the original video
- Replay support with summary substitution capsules

### Out Of Scope

- Bulk scraping of arbitrary channels
- Full transcript storage or export
- Model training or fine-tuning on video content
- Real-time live-stream monitoring
- Audio-only podcast ingestion
- Sentiment analysis or alpha scoring
- Automated trade signal generation
- Portfolio impact scoring based on YouTube sentiment
- Social media monitoring beyond YouTube
- Video content moderation or fact-checking
- Subscription management beyond user channel selection
- Video recommendation beyond subscribed-channel context
- Per-channel poll frequency (default: global configurable interval)
- Semantic video analysis (image/audio processing)

## Release / Evidence Gate

- **What can the user or operator do now that they could not before this epic?**
  Browse and inspect audited YouTube research evidence alongside trading proposals. Trace any claim in a proposal back to a specific video and timestamp.

- **What changed from the previous roadmap increment?**
  M8 established the approved execution sandbox for financial actions. V9.27 adds the first external-source research ingestion capability, closing the research → analysis → proposal → approval loop end to end.

- **What is this epic's wow-effect?**
  An operator reviews a trade proposal and can click through to the exact YouTube video, see the creator's timestamped commentary, and verify the summary is accurate and bounded — all within the same governed harness that enforces policy, audit, and replay. *No competitor can offer this. Not because they can't build a YouTube scraper — anyone can. Because they cannot build a hash-chained, replayable, policy-gated research signal.*

- **How does this epic progress Tickoni's unique value proposition?**
  Demonstrates that Tickoni's governance (capability envelopes, audit chains, replay substitution, model gateway) applies uniformly to external research sources, not just internal event streams. Every YouTube evidence source is traceable, replayable, and policy-gated — a generic scraper cannot provide this. The UVP is not "we ingest YouTube." The UVP is "everything we ingest behaves the same way: governed, audited, replayable."

- **Which demo command or CaseOps flow closes the epic?**
  Authenticate → select channels → poll → ingest → view in CaseOps → replay.

- **Which account, beneficiary, IBAN, wallet, rail, currency, market, venue, asset class, instrument, notional, amount, exposure, and frequency checks are enforced, if any?**
  Account: user's OAuth-scoped YouTube subscription scope. Market/instrument/sector tags extracted from video content for context. No money-impacting dimensions.

- **What happens when the requested action exceeds policy, scope, or evidence?**
  Capability check returns `deny` for non-subscribed channels or transcript-storage requests. Metadata-only fallback with disclaimer when captions are unavailable. API failure skips polling and logs — no fallback to unofficial scraping.

- **Is execution paper-only, draft-only, sandbox, live, or disabled?**
  Disabled. YouTube ingest never executes financial actions.

- **Which fixtures, samples, audit records, replay capsules, screenshots, or API examples prove the behavior?**
  Deterministic fixtures with mocked YouTube Data API responses. Replay capsules containing summary text and metadata. Audit records with hash chains.

- **Can replay run without external side effects when replay applies?**
  Yes. Summary substitution capsules eliminate all YouTube API calls during replay.

- **What intentional blocked-flow or divergence example proves fail-closed behavior?**
  Non-subscribed channel monitoring request → policy `deny`. YouTube API failure → poll skipped, logged. Captions unavailable → metadata-only summary with disclaimer.
