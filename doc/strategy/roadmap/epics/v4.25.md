# V4.25: Proposal-Grade Investment Verdicts

**Labels:** `type/epic`, `area/investing`, `area/platform`
**GitHub Issue:** [18](https://github.com/deeprnd/tickoni/issues/18)

V4.25 closes the loop. A valuation is not a decision until it is translated to
per-share value, compared to market price, and rendered as a recommendation with
upside and margin of safety. V4.25 takes the output of the valuation pipeline
(enterprise value, uncertainty bands, cross-checks, sensitivity) and produces a
`valuation.analysis.propose` capability record with explicit scope, policy gates,
evidence, and replay stability. The operator sees: enterprise value, uncertainty
bands, verdict (buy/hold/avoid), upside percentage, margin of safety, policy
decision, and evidence. This is the moment where analysis becomes governed output.

Institutions sit on mountains of valuation data — enterprise value, DCF
estimates, Monte Carlo uncertainty bands, sensitivity tables — but that data
does not become a decision until something bridges the gap between "we have a
valuation" and "here's what to do about it." V4.25 closes that gap. It is the
last piece of M4, wrapping all prior computation work into a governed,
policy-checked, proposal-grade output that the CaseOps operator can review and
act on.

## Product Intent

**Why:** Institutions hold the output of a complete valuation pipeline but cannot
use it. Enterprise value, uncertainty bands, and cross-checks are numbers on a
slide, not a thing a portfolio operator can review, approve, or deny. The gap
between a number and a decision is expensive to fill with manual process.

**What:** V4.25 translates enterprise value into per-share value, compares it to
market price, and produces a `valuation.analysis.propose` capability record with
explicit scope, policy gates, evidence, and replay stability. The verdict
(buy/hold/avoid), upside percentage, and margin of safety appear as the single
operational fact — everything else is supporting evidence.

**Product Promise:** A complete DCF valuation runs from data ingestion through
Monte Carlo uncertainty to a policy-gated investment verdict, with full audit
trail and forensic replay — no manual reconciliation between valuation outputs
and investment recommendations.

## Users And Jobs

- Portfolio analyst: get a valuation verdict — buy, hold, or avoid — with
  upside percentage, margin of safety, and supporting evidence.
- Risk reviewer: audit the capability envelope behind a valuation proposal,
  verify policy decisions, and inspect evidence hashes.
- CaseOps operator: review a valuation proposal card showing inputs, method,
  output range, verdict, evidence links, and policy status.
- Compliance reviewer: confirm the valuation proposal is replay-stable and
  that external effects (model calls, adapter calls) were substituted.

## Success Metrics

- Per-share value and fully diluted share count are computed from enterprise
  value and share structure.
- Investment conclusion engine produces verdict (buy/hold/avoid), upside
  percentage, and margin of safety from per-share value and market price.
- Valuation capability envelope (`valuation.analysis.propose`) enforces scope
  constraints: account, asset class, market, sector, environment.
- Audit trail captures inputs, computation results, policy decisions, and
  proposal hash.
- Deterministic replay substitutes captured results; no external effects.
- `just val-demo` produces a complete demo from data ingestion through
  conclusion.

## Scope

### In Scope

- Per-share value computation: enterprise value to per-share, fully diluted
  share count handling.
- Investment conclusion engine: verdict (buy/hold/avoid), upside, margin
  of safety.
- Valuation capability envelope: `valuation.analysis.propose` capability
  with `tkpoly` integration.
- Audit trail: inputs, computation results, policy decisions, proposal hash.
- Replay closure: deterministic replay with external effects substituted.
- Demo closure: `just val-demo` integrating all epics.
- Quality gates: fixtures, evidence, documentation.
- CaseOps visibility: valuation results surface through `tkapi` queries.

### Out Of Scope

- Automated thesis generation or recommendation — the engine produces
  evidence and a verdict; human review is required for execution.
- Model provider integration (LLMs) for valuation — the math is deterministic.
- Live pricing data integration — fixture-based.
- Portfolio-level optimization or allocation.
- Execution via `tkexec` — approval gates future execution, not this epic.
- New `tkapi` endpoints — uses existing query surface.

## Story Breakdown

### How Many Stories

Small scoped epic — 4 stories covering per-share computation, conclusion
engine, policy integration, and audit/replay/demo closure.

### Decomposition Rules

1. Independent delivery: each story produces a testable output slice.
2. One capability per story: per-share, conclusion, policy, audit.
3. Split by capability lifecycle: computation -> conclusion -> policy -> audit.
4. Closure via T10 child tasks in the final story.
5. Domain boundaries: computation, policy, audit are separate stories.

### Bad Splits (Avoid)

- "Per-share value" and "share count" as two stories — both compute the
  same output fact, same story.
- "Verdict" and "margin of safety" as two stories — both are conclusion
  outputs, same story.
- "Policy check" and "policy envelope" as two stories — policy integration
  is one capability, same story.

## Dependencies And Decisions

- V4.32 (core DCF engine) must be complete for enterprise value input.
- V4.34 (data layer) must be complete for canonical fact input.
- V4.24 (Monte Carlo) must be complete for uncertainty bands input.
- Decision: verdict thresholds — what upside percentage constitutes buy/hold/avoid.
  Suggested: buy > 20% upside, hold 0–20%, avoid < 0%. Configurable per account.
- Decision: margin of safety calculation — per-share value minus market price,
  as percentage of market price. Standard academic approach.
- Depends on `tkpoly` existing `valuation.analysis.propose` capability (to be
  defined in V4.25.S3).
- Depends on `tkrepl` for replay substitution (existing infrastructure).
