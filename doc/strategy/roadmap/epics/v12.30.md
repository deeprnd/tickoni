# V12.30: Social Thesis And Money Feed

**Status:** Later
**Milestone:** M9
**Labels:** `epic`, `area/investing`, `area/payments`, `area/social`, `area/trust`

Allow users to browse thesis cards and money-decision cards, inspect risk
checks, and copy investing or payment templates into their own accounts with
their own limits.

## Product Intent

The user sees someone else's thesis or payment template and wants to apply it
to their own account. The product must copy the structure while resizing to
the user's real constraints. This is the social-share surface for Tickoni's
finance-native templates: investing theses and payment patterns, published as
cards that anyone can browse and copy — always resized to the copier's own
limits.

## Users And Jobs

- [Retail investor]: browse public thesis cards, inspect risk checks, copy a
  thesis into their portfolio with their own buying power and policy
- [Retail payer]: browse public payment-template cards, inspect risk notes,
  copy a transfer or payment pattern into their approved beneficiaries
- [Operator]: moderate or curate the public card feed (curation surface, out
  of current scope)

## Success Metrics

- A user can copy a thesis card into their portfolio with zero manual
  constraint entry
- A user can copy a payment-template card into their beneficiary list with
  zero manual constraint entry
- Copying never bypasses the user's own account, instrument, venue, sector,
  beneficiary, notional, or concentration limits
- Resized copies explain which constraints changed and why

## Demo Moment

- Command or flow: browse a published thesis card in CaseOps (or terminal),
  click "copy to my portfolio", select the target portfolio, submit
- Expected result: a new portfolio holding appears with the same basket and
  rationale as the original, but notional, concentration, and frequency limits
  reflect the user's policy, and the UI shows which fields were resized and
  why
- Command or flow: browse a published payment-template card, click
  "copy to my recipients", submit
- Expected result: approved beneficiary entries appear with the template's
  rail, amount range, and risk notes, constrained by the user's own recipient
  and notional limits

## Scope

### In Scope

- Public thesis card schema (basket, rationale, historical paper performance,
  risk notes)
- Public payment-template card schema (rail, amount range, risk notes,
  evidence requirements, recipient masked)
- Shareable thesis snapshot endpoint
- Shareable payment-template endpoint
- Copy-to-my-portfolio action with constraint resizing
- Copy-to-my-recipient action for approved beneficiaries only
- Explanation of differences between original and copied version
- Hard block on bypassing account, instrument, venue, sector, beneficiary,
  wallet, notional, amount, or concentration limits

### Out Of Scope

- Unbounded copy trading
- Creator-driven auto-execution
- Influencer marketplace mechanics or revenue sharing
- Copying untrusted recipients or wallets
- Card publishing or moderation tooling (publishing workflow)
- Social features (follow, like, comment)
- Analytics on card views or copy rates
- Mobile or native app surfaces

## Release / Evidence Gate

- What can the user or operator do now that they could not before this epic?
  Browse and copy other users' thesis and payment templates, resized to their
  own limits.
- What changed from the previous roadmap increment?
  Adds a social-share surface and constraint-aware copy workflow on top of
  existing portfolio and recipient management.
- What is this epic's wow-effect?
  A user copies a thesis card with one click and sees the basket appear in
  their portfolio — already constrained to their limits, with a diff showing
  exactly what changed.
- How does this epic progress Tickoni's unique value proposition?
  Speed and isolation: shareable templates that auto-enforce the user's own
  policy boundaries. Spend governance: resized notional, concentration, and
  frequency caps apply on copy. Policy-gated action: the copy is a proposal,
  not execution. Forensic replay: copy flows are deterministic and auditable.
- Which demo command or CaseOps flow closes the epic?
  CaseOps card browse → copy → policy resize → diff explanation → audit record.
- Which account, beneficiary, notional, and limit checks are enforced?
  Account scope, instrument/venue/sector allowlists, beneficiary allowlists,
  wallet allowlists, notional caps, concentration caps, frequency limits,
  all from the user's own capability envelope.
- What happens when the requested action exceeds policy, scope, or evidence?
  `tkpoly` returns `deny` or `require_approval`; the copy is blocked or
  routed to human review with the reason visible.
- Is execution paper-only, draft-only, sandbox, live, or disabled?
  Paper-only — proposals only, no execution.
- Which fixtures, samples, or audit records prove the behavior?
  A sample thesis card, a sample payment-template card, a resized copy output,
  and the audit record for the copy action.
- Can replay run without external side effects when replay applies?
  Yes — replay substitutes the source card snapshot and user policy, reproduces
  the resize decision, and outputs the same explanation.
- What intentional blocked-flow or divergence example proves fail-closed behavior?
  Attempting to copy a thesis that includes a sector or instrument outside the
  user's allowlist is denied; the copy does not partially apply.
